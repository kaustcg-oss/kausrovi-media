A Class I heart-pump controller recall, guidewire coatings that may detach and breathing-tube connectors

The week in 60 seconds

Recall of the week: Abiomed Automated Impella Controllers, Class I

DATA: event 99778, posted October 28, 2026 (initiated September 18, 2026). Recalling firm: Abiomed, Inc., Danvers, MA. Products: Automated Impella Controller, Impella Optical Controller and Impella Connect Optical AIC (all “Packaged”), the primary user control interface for the Impella catheter-based heart pump, in 3 product records (product code OZD, device name “Temporary Non-Roller Type Left Heart Support Blood Pump”; PMA P140003 and P170011; recall numbers Z-0208-2027 to Z-0210-2027; 428, 179 and 293 units, 900 in total). Reason as written in the record: “Retrospective review of servicing records identified AIC units requiring various hardware, firmware, and/or software update(s)”: an RFID firmware issue that may cause the controller not to recognize the purge cassette during case setup or cassette change, which may delay procedure or therapy; a rotary push button that may stick, which “can result in loss of control of the console”; controller instability that could cause start-up failure, inability to drive the pump and the need for console exchange; and a software defect in which a calculator process crash leads to negative central venous pressure values in pulmonary artery pressure calculations. Root cause as written: under investigation by firm. Class: Class I (openFDA device/enforcement, classified October 28, 2026; report date November 4). According to the record, Abiomed sent “URGENT MEDICAL DEVICE RECALL (REMOVAL)” letters on about September 18, 2026; affected units can continue to be used while awaiting service, to ensure continuity of care; Abiomed will contact each consignee to coordinate removal, update and return; consignees should identify units on hand, return the business reply form, inform staff and notify any facility that received forwarded units. Distribution: worldwide, U.S. nationwide and 31 other countries listed, including Mexico. FDA’s November 9 notice, an update to a September 23 early alert, says FDA has identified this as the most serious type of recall, lists U.S. product numbers 0042-0000-US (specific consoles), 0042-0010-US and 0042-0040-US, and says that as of September 8, 2026 Abiomed reported no serious injuries or deaths. The record’s contact line names an individual, so we do not reproduce it; the record’s action text gives ra-abm-fieldaction@its.jnj.com.

ANALYSIS: the record’s own root cause is still “under investigation”, and the units stay in use until Abiomed services them, so cath labs and cardiac ICUs may want to confirm which consoles are affected and that the firm has scheduled the update.

Chosen by a fixed rule (most serious class, then most product records); it is not a judgment that this is the most important recall. No events were posted November 9–15, so the rule ran on events posted October 26 to November 5; Abiomed 99778 is the only Class I event among them. 1. Arrow International: early alert on guidewire components whose coating may detach

DATA: FDA issued this early alert on November 10, 2026. Arrow International, a subsidiary of Teleflex Incorporated, is removing products that contain spring wire guide components with a hydrophilic coating: hydrophilic-coated nitinol and stainless-steel guidewires, the Arrow VPS Precision Stylet, and pressure-injectable PICCs and JACCs, many with 80 cm or 130 cm nitinol guidewires and some sold as pre-loaded kits (FDA links the full product list). Teleflex confirmed that the coating “may swell inconsistently when hydrated with saline leading to clumping of the coating and potential for detachment.” FDA lists possible harms from hydrophilic polymer emboli, including inflammation, thrombosis, ischemia, tissue injury, stroke, further intervention or death. As of November 5, 2026, Arrow reported no serious injuries or deaths. Arrow’s November 5 letter asks customers to check inventory, stop using and distributing affected products, quarantine and return them, and pass the notice to any customers who received them. The page states no class. Company contact on the page: Recalls@teleflex.com, 1-866-396-2111.

ANALYSIS: the affected items include components inside kits and PICC/JACC sets, so inventory checks may need to go below the box level to the components listed by FDA.

Source, accessed 2026-11-16: FDA early alert, Arrow International

2. Intersurgical: early alert on 22mm Smoothbore breathing tubes and systems

DATA: FDA issued this early alert on November 10, 2026. Intersurgical Inc. is removing 11 product references (38 lots) of 22mm Smoothbore tubes and breathing systems, including tubes with soft end connectors, heated-wire single and dual breathing systems, systems with monitoring lines and exhalation valves, and bilevel systems with CO2 leak ports; the largest group is REF 5805030 with 18 lots. The firm uses tetrahydrofuran (THF) solvent to bond soft connectors to the tube; after reports from outside the U.S. of connectors detaching, testing found some products were made with “too little solvent to ensure a secure connection.” A detached connector could interrupt or compromise ventilation; FDA says that, depending on the patient and the length of the interruption, this could lead to organ failure, brain damage or death from hypoxia. As of November 3, 2026, the firm reported no serious injuries or deaths. Its November 3 letter asks customers to stop using and quarantine affected lots, return them, share the notice with all users and contact any customers they supplied. The page states no class. Company contact on the page: Intersurgical Customer Service, recalls@intersurgicalinc.com, 800-828-9633.

ANALYSIS: like the B. Braun bloodline alert in Issue 04, this is a bonding step that did not get enough solvent, so lot numbers, not product names, are what respiratory and anesthesia departments need to match.

Source, accessed 2026-11-16: FDA early alert, Intersurgical

3. Luminex: blood-culture panel instructions updated for family-level results

DATA: event 100036, posted November 5, 2026 (initiated October 5, 2026), loaded after Issue 05’s data cut-off. Luminex Corporation, Northbrook, IL. Product: LIAISON PLEX Gram-Negative Blood Culture Assay, catalog 20-005-303, all lots (product code NSU; 510(k) K243013; recall number Z-0359-2027; 30,168 test cartridges, 2,514 kits). Reason as written: “Instructions for Use updated to include explicit guidance that repeat testing is recommended whenever a positive family-level only result is obtained. Issue may contribute to incorrect or delayed diagnosis of bloodstream infection.” Root cause: error in labeling. Per the record, “Urgent Field Safety Notice” letters went out from October 5; customers are asked to apply the new guidance now (repeat testing with the assay when only a family-level result is positive), and revised labeling will follow. Distribution: U.S. nationwide, no distribution outside the U.S. Class: not yet in openFDA device/enforcement. Company contact in the record: Technical Support, 877-785-2323 ext. 9029.

ANALYSIS: this is an interpretation change rather than a product defect, so microbiology labs using the panel may want to update their result-review procedures before the revised IFU arrives.

Source, accessed 2026-11-16: openFDA device/recall, event 100036

ANÁLISIS: según la nota, se trata de capacidad de apoyo a la manufactura (herramental), no de una nueva línea de producto.
