Issue 02 · Week of October 12–18, 2026

Respiratory-panel contamination, a patient-lift motor shaft and a disinfectant vent cap

Informational content prepared from public sources. Not legal or regulatory advice. Verify all data at the official source. All sources accessed 2026-10-18. Leer en español

The week in 60 seconds

  • At least five device recall events were posted by the FDA on October 13–14: BioFire Diagnostics (respiratory and joint-infection panels), Baxter (patient lifts), Steris (a high-level disinfectant), Ethicon (a surgical suture) and In2bones (bone screws).
  • The BioFire record describes possible contamination of specific lots that could cause false-positive results; the firm lists the root cause as under investigation.
  • The FDA warning-letter table showed ten letters posted October 13; none came from CDRH, the device center.

How to read this issue. openFDA device/recall data runs through 2026-10-15, so events posted October 16–18 may not appear yet. openFDA reports 10 records for the window and we could read 9 in full. We did not verify recall classes (I, II, III) at publication, so this issue does not state them. Because of that, we cannot apply our usual “recall of the week” selection rule this week; recalls are listed with the largest events first (most product records, then earliest posting), and that is not a judgment of importance. 1. BioFire Diagnostics: possible contamination in respiratory and joint-infection panels

DATA: event 99592, posted October 13, 2026 (firm initiated August 12, 2026). Recalling firm: BioFire Diagnostics, LLC, Salt Lake City, UT. Products: FilmArray Pneumonia Panel, FilmArray Pneumonia Panel plus and BioFire Joint Infection Panel (product codes QDP, QDS and QSN; 4,892, 3,871 and 1,186 units). Reason as written in the record: possible contamination of specific lots, which could cause false-positive results. Root cause: under investigation by the firm. According to the record, a September 3, 2026 urgent correction letter reported elevated unexpected KPC positives in quality-control testing, likely from KPC nucleic acid contamination in reagent manufacturing. Customers are asked to check inventory, confirm unexpected KPC results with an alternative method, notify recipients, post the notice and return an acknowledgment form. Distribution: worldwide, including nationwide in the U.S.

ANALYSIS: for labs running multiplex panels, the record points to one practical question: were unexpected positives for a specific target confirmed by a second method?

Source: openFDA device/recall, posted 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-18 (event 99592)

2. Steris: blocked vent cap on a high-level disinfectant

DATA: event 99804, posted October 14, 2026 (initiated September 21, 2026). Steris Corporation, Mentor, OH. RAPICIDE PA Part A high-level disinfectant, models ML020115, ML020116 and ML020117 (product code FEB; 374, 11,806 and 106,758 units). Reason as written: a blocked vent cap can cause gas buildup and bottle swelling; opening the cap may release liquid or vapor, which could expose users if personal protective equipment is not used. Root cause: process change control. Per the record, an urgent letter dated September 8, 2026 says product may be used through its shelf life; if a bottle is bulging, users must wear protective equipment, vent the pressure slowly, dispose of the product and return a response form for replacement.

ANALYSIS: the record keeps the product in use with handling precautions rather than ordering it out of service.

Source: openFDA device/recall, posted 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-18 (event 99804) Logo: trademark of its owner, shown only to identify the company.

3. Baxter: Likorall overhead patient lifts

DATA: event 99802, initiated September 14, 2026. Baxter Healthcare Corporation, Deerfield, IL. Likorall overhead patient lifts (models include 200, 242 ES, 243 ES, 250 ES and 250 S; product code FSA). Reason as written: a motor shaft may break, and reduced performance of the Single Fault Safety system can cause the lift strap to lower faster than expected, creating a fall or drop injury risk. Root cause: process control. Per the record, correction letters were mailed to facilities starting September 14, 2026 and home-patient notices followed on September 15; Baxter will inspect and correct devices and recommends a mobile lift as an interim alternative. Distribution: worldwide, including nationwide in the U.S. The record does not give a quantity.

ANALYSIS: the correction is carried out by the manufacturer on site, so the first task for a facility is knowing which lift models it has.

Source: openFDA device/recall, posted 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-18 (event 99802)

4. Ethicon: PERMA-HAND Silk suture packaged with alcohol-based fluid

DATA: event 99754, posted October 14, 2026 (initiated September 8, 2026). Ethicon, LLC, San Lorenzo (PR). PERMA-HAND Silk black braided sterile nonabsorbable surgical suture, 1x18", TF-5, code N271H (product code GAQ; 1,764 units). Reason as written: the sutures were packaged with an alcohol-based fluid that is not part of the design, which could weaken the suture or knot, cause tissue irritation or lead to treatment failure. Root cause: process control. Per the record, notices were mailed September 8, 2026; customers must quarantine product, notify relevant staff, return product with a response form and forward the notice to other facilities.

ANALYSIS: three of this week’s five events list a process-related root cause (process control or process change control); one is a labeling error and one is under investigation. Five events do not support a generalization.

Source: openFDA device/recall, posted 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-18 (event 99754) Logo: trademark of its owner, shown only to identify the company.

5. In2bones: screws labeled “fully threaded” are partially threaded

DATA: event 99846, posted October 14, 2026 (initiated September 9, 2026). In2bones USA, LLC, Cordova, TN. CoLag screw, fully threaded, 6.7x62.5 mm, reference P65 ST862 (product code HWC; 29 units). Reason as written: a labeling error; screws labeled as fully threaded are actually partially threaded. Root cause: error in labeling. Per the record, an urgent notice was issued September 9, 2026; consignees must quarantine and stop using affected devices, share the notice, and return devices and a response form (opened or used devices should not be returned). Distribution: nine U.S. states (FL, GA, HI, NC, OH, PA, SD, TX, VA).

ANALYSIS: a mismatch between label and product, a failure type that labeling verification and incoming inspection are meant to catch.

Source: openFDA device/recall, posted 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-18 (event 99846)

Warning letters: none from CDRH in the latest posting

DATA: the FDA warning-letter table, read on October 18, 2026, listed ten letters posted October 13, 2026: six from CDER (drugs), two from the Center for Veterinary Medicine, one from the Center for Tobacco Products and one from the Human Foods Program. None came from CDRH, the device center.

ANALYSIS: no device warning letter appears in this posting. The device letters we covered in Issue 01 were posted on October 6.
