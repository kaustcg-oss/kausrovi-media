Philips angiography drives, Medline beds and a HIT test that reads low

The week in 60 seconds

Recall of the week: Philips Azurion systems, unspecified solid-state drives

DATA: event 99939, posted October 27, 2026 (initiated October 6, 2026). Recalling firm: Philips Medical Systems Nederland B.V., Best, Netherlands. Products: Philips Azurion 3 M12 and M15, Azurion 5 M12 and M20, Azurion 7 B12, B20, M12 and M20, and Azurion S, in 9 product records (product code OWB, device name “Interventional Fluoroscopic X-Ray System”; recall numbers Z-0292-2027 to Z-0300-2027; per-record quantities from 1 to 1,896 units, 4,304 in total). Reason as written in the record: solid-state drives (SSDs) not specified in the Medical Device File may have been installed in various PCs; they may have different performance characteristics, which may include reduced reliability over time such as loss of imaging functionality or motorized movement, a blank or black display, and/or the inability to store newly acquired images or access previously acquired images. Root cause as written: equipment maintenance. Class: not verified (see above). According to the record, consignees were notified on about October 6, 2026 by certified “URGENT Medical Device Correction” letter; affected systems may continue to be used under the instructions for use and the added instructions, including manual stand repositioning if motorized movement becomes unavailable; consignees are asked to distribute and post the letter near affected devices and return the response form. Philips will inspect all systems in scope and replace non-specified SSDs, and expects to start in December 2026. Distribution: worldwide, including U.S. nationwide and Mexico. The record gives no company contact line.

ANALYSIS: the record keeps these systems in use until a field visit, so the practical first step for an interventional suite is confirming which Azurion models it runs and that staff know the manual-positioning instructions.

Chosen by a fixed rule (most serious class, then most product records); it is not a judgment that this is the most important recall. Classes were not available this week, so the rule picked the event with the most product records (9). Other events in the window: Pentax of America (5 records), Abiomed (3), Canon Medical System USA (3), Medtronic (3), Philips North America (1), bioMérieux (1). 1. Medline: early alert on all Medline-brand beds, two deaths reported

DATA: FDA issued this early alert on October 29, 2026. It covers all Medline-brand beds and associated pendants, accessories, replacement parts and bed-containing order packages, including homecare, bariatric and long-term care beds. FDA describes two hazards: electrical components that may overheat, leading to smoke, sparking, melting, burning or fire; and entrapment or asphyxiation when accessories are used, when a user lies on a pendant, or when rails are loose. As of October 20, 2026, Medline reported two deaths and 16 serious injuries. Medline sent a field safety notice on October 14 on removal, discontinuation and market exit, and an updated notice on October 24. Per the alert, beds may stay in use until repaired or replaced under the updated instructions: unplug the bed and plug it in only while repositioning the patient, use no extension cords, and do not repair or modify beds outside an authorized process. The alert states no class for this action. It links two earlier Medline events, 99827 and 98185, which openFDA device/enforcement lists as Class I. Company contact on the page: Medline Recall Customer Service, 1-800-950-0128, option 7.

ANALYSIS: because the action covers a whole brand, facilities and homecare suppliers may need to match their assets against FDA’s linked product list before anything else.

Sources, accessed 2026-11-02: FDA early alert, Medline · openFDA device/enforcement, event 99827 · event 98185 (openFDA device/enforcement data through 2026-10-21)

2. Werfen: HIT test lots that may read falsely negative

DATA: on October 30, 2026 FDA posted a recall notice for HemosIL AcuStar HIT-IgG(PF4-H) from Instrumentation Laboratories (Werfen), an immunoassay used to help assess the risk of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia, and says it has identified this as the most serious type of recall. Affected: part 0009800127, lots B40005 and B38062; part 0009802028, lots B40006 and B38063. Werfen found a negative bias of 20–35% in these lots; complaints reported invalid calibration curves, and one reported a 47% negative mean bias with patient samples. FDA says routine calibration and quality control may not reliably detect the problem, and that results may fall below the 1.00 U/mL cut-off, delaying HIT diagnosis. The root-cause investigation is ongoing. As of September 3, per the page, Werfen reported no serious injuries or deaths. Customers are asked to destroy remaining product, document it on the tracking form and identify an alternative test because a supply interruption is expected. openFDA device/enforcement lists event 99843 as Class I. Company contact on the page: Werfen Client Services, 1-800-955-9525.

ANALYSIS: the notice leaves any review of earlier results to the laboratory or treating clinician, so labs that ran these lots may want that decision documented.

Sources, accessed 2026-11-02: FDA recall notice, HemosIL AcuStar · openFDA device/enforcement, event 99843

3. B. Braun: dialysis bloodline connector that may detach

DATA: FDA issued this early alert on October 27, 2026. Product: Streamline Bloodline Set for Dialog+, model SL-2010M2096, lots A2600099, A2600128 and A2600136. B. Braun says a required solvent application step was skipped during assembly, so the dialyzer connector did not bond properly and may detach or leak during priming or dialysis. Listed potential harms include blood loss, clotting of the circuit and, in extremely rare cases, air embolism. No injuries or deaths reported as of October 10, 2026. B. Braun’s October 20 letter asks customers to determine inventory, stop using and quarantine affected product, return it, and for distributors to forward the notice. The page gives no class. Company contact on the page: recalls@bbraunusa.com, (610) 849-1197.

ANALYSIS: the action is lot-specific, so clinics can check it against inventory directly.

Source, accessed 2026-11-02: FDA early alert, B. Braun

4. Nova Biomedical: FDA clarifies actions on a Class I hospital glucose meter recall

DATA: FDA first posted this Class I communication on October 20, 2026 and updated it October 30 “to clarify the recommended actions”. Product: StatStrip Glucose Hospital Meter System, Gen 2.0, models 63685 and 66739, all firmware versions. When scanning patient ID or quality-control barcodes, information from a previous scan can remain on screen; if accepted, a glucose result may be filed to the wrong patient. As of October 5, 2026, Nova reported one serious injury and no deaths. While awaiting software, FDA lists steps including enabling “Validate Patient ID’s” where ADT integration is used, setting operator logoff to “Test”, checking identifiers before accepting a scan, and stopping use if an alternative meter is available. Nova plans a phased software rollout starting in November 2026 and has placed a shipment hold on new Gen 2.0 units. openFDA device/enforcement lists event 99546 as Class I. Company contact on the page: Nova Biomedical, 1-800-545-6682.

ANALYSIS: FDA says analytical performance is not affected; this is a patient-identification failure, which makes it a useful case for reviewing use-error and data-integrity risk in point-of-care devices.

Sources, accessed 2026-11-02: FDA notice, Nova Biomedical · openFDA device/enforcement, event 99546

5. Three CDRH warning letters posted October 27

DATA: the FDA table lists these CDRH letters posted October 27, 2026. Chirp Innovations, LLC, Lehi, UT (letter October 2, 2026; inspection November 18–21, 2025; Chirp Halo Wireless Muscle Stimulator): undocumented quality-system procedures under the QS Regulation (21 CFR 820.20, 820.22, 820.30, 820.50, 820.100, 820.198), written medical device reporting procedures (21 CFR 803.17), and promotion for menstrual pain that FDA says falls outside the cleared indications. Peter Schiff Enterprises, Cookeville, TN (letter August 26, 2026; inspection December 29–31, 2025; cardiac electrode pads and a fibrillator): no approved PMA or IDE and no 510(k) notice, per FDA, plus quality-system items, reporting procedures (21 CFR 803.17) and UDI/GUDID items. Pro-Optics, LLC, Palatine, IL (letter October 5, 2026): see QMSR watch. Each letter asks for a written response within 15 business days.

ANALYSIS: all three pair a premarket question with quality-system items, which is a reminder that labeling and change decisions get read alongside the QMS. We did not review the firms’ responses or any later FDA action.

Sources, accessed 2026-11-02: Chirp Innovations · Peter Schiff Enterprises · FDA warning-letter table

QMSR watch

DATA: Pro-Optics, LLC, Palatine, IL. Letter dated October 5, 2026, posted October 27; inspection March 3–11, 2026; Tight Top and Flip Top contact lens cases. The letter cites the Quality Management System Regulation (21 CFR Part 820) and two clauses: ISO 13485:2016 Clause 7.3.1 (failure to document procedures for design and development) and Clause 4.1.4 (failure to control or evaluate changes to quality management system processes). It also says the firm changed the polymers and colorants of the cases cleared under K875197 without a new 510(k) (21 CFR 807.81(a)(3)(i)). The page header shows CMS 734689 while the letter body and response instructions show CMS 739966. The other two CDRH letters in this posting, Chirp Innovations and Peter Schiff Enterprises, stem from 2025 inspections and use the old QS Regulation section numbers.

ANALYSIS: clause 4.1.4 does not appear in the letters we listed in Issues 01 and 03, but one letter is a small sample, not a trend. Informational only; not legal or regulatory advice.

Source, accessed 2026-11-02: FDA warning letter, Pro-Optics, LLC
