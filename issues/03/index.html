Recall of the week: Ventec ventilators, possible connector disconnection

DATA: event 99835, posted October 20, 2026 (initiated September 17, 2026). Recalling firm: Ventec Life Systems, Inc., Bothell, WA (the record’s action text says Ventec dba React Health). Products: eight ventilator models and their Japan versions, listed in 12 product records: VOCSN+Pro, VC+Pro, VOCSN, VCSN+Pro, V+Pro, VC, V+ Pro Emergency and V*Home (product code CBK, device name “Ventilator, Continuous, Facility Use”; recall numbers Z-0122-2027 to Z-0133-2027; per-record quantities from 100 to 39,568 units). Reason as written in the record: the ventilator’s internal flow transducer cable connector may disconnect from the internal board, potentially resulting in a system reboot and interruption of ventilation therapy. Root cause as written: under investigation by the firm. According to the record, an urgent recall notification was sent by certified mail on September 17, 2026; consignees are asked to follow the actions in the notice, return the customer response form within 7 days, and notify anyone to whom they passed on affected devices. Distribution: U.S. nationwide including Puerto Rico. Company contact in the record: React Health, 844-698-6276.

ANALYSIS: the record describes a hardware connection that can interrupt therapy, so the practical first step for a facility or home-care provider is identifying which of the listed models it holds. The root cause is still open in the record.

Chosen by a fixed rule (most serious class, then most product records); it is not a judgment that this is the most important recall. Classes were not available this week, so the rule picked the event with the most product records (12). Other events in the window: Beckman Coulter (2 records), Greiner Bio-One (1), Medtronic MiniMed (1). 1. Greiner Bio-One: possible EDTA contamination in one lot of blood-collection tubes

DATA: event 99518, posted October 21, 2026 (initiated July 29, 2026). Greiner Bio-One North America, Inc., Monroe, NC. VACUETTE LH Lithium Heparin Separator tubes, 13 x 75 mm, 3.5 ml, item 454008P, lot B26043FM (product code JKA; 860,450 as written in the record). Reason as written: the lot may include EDTA contamination, which adds potassium and binds calcium in patient samples, leading to falsely elevated potassium and falsely decreased calcium results with the potential to cause hyperkalemia. Root cause: nonconforming material/component. Per the record, an urgent notice was emailed on July 29, 2026; customers are asked to quarantine the lot, stop using it, notify relevant staff, review recent patient results from the lot and consider repeat testing with a different lot. Distribution: U.S. nationwide in CO, FL, IL, MO, NE, NY, OH, PA and VA.

ANALYSIS: the failure shows up in lab results, not on the device, so reviewing results from the lot matters as much as quarantining stock.

Source, accessed 2026-10-24: openFDA device/recall, event 99518

2. Medtronic MiniMed: CareLink Clinic calculator ignores custom insulin values

DATA: event 99765, posted October 21, 2026 (initiated September 11, 2026). Medtronic MiniMed, Inc., Northridge, CA. CareLink Clinic software, REF MMT-7350, version 4.4A (product code PHV; device name “Continuous Glucose Monitor Retrospective Data Analysis Software”; quantity 1 as written). Reason as written: in the Settings Calculator, the logic does not use custom Total Daily Dose or long-acting insulin values entered by healthcare professionals when calculating carb ratio, insulin sensitivity factor and the maximum recommended rapid-acting value, and keeps using the system-recommended values; if those settings are entered into a patient’s MiniMed Go app, insulin doses could be higher or lower than needed. The record rates the possible severity from minor to catastrophic (death from severe hypoglycemia). Root cause: software design. Per the record, the software was available in the U.S. June 4–8, 2026, and correction notices were mailed September 11, 2026; patients are asked to confirm their settings with their provider, and clinicians to revisit recommendations based on custom values entered in that period. Distribution: U.S. nationwide distribution in the state of KS, as written.

ANALYSIS: the record limits the exposure to a few days of availability and one unit, which makes this a narrow case, but it is a clear example of a software defect that ignores a user input.

Source, accessed 2026-10-24: openFDA device/recall, event 99765

3. Beckman Coulter: ultracentrifuge rotors that may break during a run

DATA: event 99882, posted October 21, 2026 (initiated September 21, 2026). Beckman Coulter, Inc., Indianapolis, IN. ROTOR ASSY-SW40 (reference 331302; 92 units) and ROTOR ASSY-SW41 (reference 331362; 336 units), used in Optima XPN and Optima XE ultracentrifuges (product code JQC). Reason as written: a microstructure defect can cause rotors to break into two or more pieces during a run, destroying the samples being processed; for diagnostic sample preparation, breakage may delay patient results, and sample destruction may expose laboratory personnel to biohazardous material. Root cause: nonconforming material/component. Per the record, a letter dated September 9, 2026 asks customers to return the response form for a free replacement; if another compatible rotor is available, use it meanwhile; if not, continued use is possible until replacement but carries a possibility of breakage, and personal protective equipment should be used when opening the centrifuge after a breakage. Distribution: U.S. nationwide.

ANALYSIS: the record leaves the decision to keep using an affected rotor to the lab, so labs should know which rotor serial numbers they run and whether a compatible alternative is on hand.

Source, accessed 2026-10-24: openFDA device/recall, event 99882

4. Warning letter: Olympus Medical Systems Corporation, under the pre-QMSR rule

DATA: the FDA table lists a CDRH letter to Olympus Medical Systems Corporation, issued October 7, 2026 and posted October 20, 2026, with the subject “CGMP/QSR/Medical Devices/Adulterated”. The letter (addressed to the company’s Hachioji, Tokyo, address) covers inspections between October 6 and November 21, 2025 at six facilities in Japan, Germany and the United States (Brooklyn Park, MN). It cites 12 items, including design validation (21 CFR 820.30(g)), control of nonconforming product (820.90(a)), process validation (820.75), CAPA (820.100), purchasing controls (820.50), complaint investigation (820.198(c)) and timeliness of medical device reports (21 CFR 803). It says the inspections were conducted under the QS Regulation, that the QMSR took effect February 2, 2026, and that corrective actions must follow the QMSR. It does not cite ISO 13485 clauses. It asks for a written response within 15 business days. The same posting also lists a letter to Tides Medical LLC, issued by CBER, with the subject IDE/PMA.

ANALYSIS: this is a letter about quality-system items, but under the old section numbers, because the inspections predate February 2026. We did not review the company’s response or any later FDA action.

Source, accessed 2026-10-24: FDA warning letter, Olympus Medical Systems Corporation (CMS #729208) · FDA warning-letter table

QMSR watch

DATA: the Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR, amended 21 CFR Part 820, which brings in ISO 13485:2016) took effect February 2, 2026. We read three CDRH warning letters on FDA.gov that cite ISO 13485 clauses. Linemaster Switch Corporation, Woodstock, CT (letter May 27, 2026; inspection February 4 to March 6, 2026): five violations, citing clauses 8.3.4, 7.1, 8.5.2, 6.4.1 and 7.6. Nipro Renal Solutions USA Corporation, Lewisberry, PA (header date July 24, 2026; letter text dated July 16; inspection February 2 to March 27, 2026): clauses 8.3.1, 8.5.2, 7.3.1, 7.3.9, 6.3, 7.6, 7.5.6, 7.1 and 7.4.1 plus UDI items; its risk item refers to Class I recall Z-1730-2025, and the page mixes QSR and ISO wording. Lion Street Medical dba Pensar Medical LLC, Houston, TX (August 21, 2026; inspection April 14–28, 2026): clauses 7.1, 7.3.9 and 8.5.2 plus medical device reporting and corrections/removals items. Across these three letters, clause 7.1 (risk management) and 8.5.2 (corrective action) appear in all three, and 7.3.9 (design-change control) in two. This week’s Olympus letter (item 4) still uses the old numbering.

ANALYSIS: three letters are a small sample, not a trend, and each firm’s situation is different. Letters tied to inspections before February 2026 still use the old section numbers, so both citation styles appear side by side in current postings. Informational only; not legal or regulatory advice.

Secondary source, not verified against FDA data: the FDA Law Blog (August 12, 2026) reports Koven Technologies (letter July 21, 2026) as another early QMSR letter and risk-management observations as the most frequent in Form 483s. Sources, accessed 2026-10-24: Linemaster Switch · Nipro Renal Solutions · Pensar Medical
