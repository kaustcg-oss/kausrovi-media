Recall of the week: Henry Schein Perma Sharp sutures, no 510(k) clearance

DATA: event 100016, posted November 3, 2026 (initiated September 17, 2026). Recalling firm: Henry Schein Inc., Denver, PA. Products: Perma Sharp sutures in 13 catalog numbers, covering polypropylene nonabsorbable, PGA (polyglycolic acid) absorbable and silk nonabsorbable sutures (for example PSN8696P, PSN392V and PSN683S), in 13 product records (product codes GAW, GAM and GAP; device names “Suture, Nonabsorbable, Synthetic, Polypropylene”, “Suture, Absorbable, Synthetic, Polyglycolic Acid” and “Suture, Nonabsorbable, Silk”; recall numbers Z-0334-2027 to Z-0346-2027; per-record quantities from 10 to 472, 1,016 in total as written, with no unit stated). Reason as written in the record: “Products lack 510(k) clearance.” Root cause as written: process control. Class: not verified (see above). According to the record, “URGENT: MEDICAL DEVICE RECALL NOTIFICATION” letters went to customers on September 25, 2026; customers are asked to check inventory, immediately stop distributing and using affected product, remove it from available inventory and return it within 30 days to Henry Schein’s returns facility in Denver, PA. Distribution: worldwide, including U.S. nationwide, Canada, Uganda and the Cayman Islands. The record’s contact line names an individual, so we do not reproduce it.

ANALYSIS: the stated reason is regulatory status, not a reported product defect, so the practical step for clinics and distributors is matching catalog and lot numbers against the record and following the return instructions.

Chosen by a fixed rule (most serious class, then most product records); it is not a judgment that this is the most important recall. Classes were not available this week, so the rule picked the event with the most product records (13). Other events in the window: Cardinal Health 200 (11 records), Baxter Healthcare, Deerfield (5), GE Medical Systems (3), Baxter Healthcare, Round Lake (2), and one record each for AVID Medical, Cochlear, Corin Medical, Edwards Lifesciences, Intelerad Medical Systems, Remel, restor3d and Shimadzu. 1. Baxter: TruSystem surgical tables whose main lift may drop

DATA: two Baxter events were posted November 3, 2026, both for operating tables (product code GDC). Event 100117 (Baxter Healthcare Corporation, Deerfield, IL; initiated September 29, 2026; recall numbers Z-0350-2027 to Z-0354-2027) covers TruSystem 7500, TS7500 MOBIUS, TruSystem 7000 U, TruSystem 7000 U (dV) and TruSystem 7500 Hybrid Plus (FC) tables and columns, 285 units in total. Reason as written: the main lift may unexpectedly drop from its set position to its lowest position during use, and the tabletop could not be raised again afterwards; the issue has been associated with unexpected wear of the main lift assembly (telescopic spindle), and an uncontrolled drop may cause abrupt patient displacement during surgery. Root cause: nonconforming material/component. Per the record’s September 29 letter, Baxter will contact facilities when a correction is available (it expects to begin by the end of 2026); meanwhile customers should monitor the table for unusual noises or changes in performance as the IFU instructs, stop using it and call Baxter Technical Service if a noise is observed, and post the notice by the device. Distribution: worldwide, including U.S. nationwide. Event 99826 (Baxter Healthcare Corporation, Round Lake, IL; initiated September 14, 2026; Z-0332-2027 and Z-0333-2027; 63 units) is separate: per the record, four units had a spindle replaced without the required belt-tension verification, which could lead to unintended downward movement of the table column; Baxter reports no patient injuries to date, will schedule a belt-tension check, and says the tables can stay in use. Root cause: equipment maintenance. Distribution: IL, MO and TX. Company contact in both records: Center for One Baxter, 1-847-948-4770.

ANALYSIS: one event is a design or component wear issue and the other a service step that was skipped, so OR and biomedical teams may want to check both lists, including any tables serviced recently.

Sources, accessed 2026-11-09: openFDA device/recall, event 100117 · event 99826

2. Compact Medical: early alert on ButterflyBVM resuscitators

DATA: FDA issued this early alert on November 3, 2026. Compact Medical reports that a hole can form in the bellows of certain ButterflyBVM bag-valve-mask resuscitators, and that lots 000109 to 000117 show a higher rate of holes than seen before. Tidal volume can leak out instead of reaching the patient, which can cause inadequate ventilation and hypoxia; FDA says this could lead to serious injury or death if not corrected. As of November 2, 2026, the firm reported no serious injuries and no deaths. Lots 000109 to 000117 (ButterflyBVM READY R300/R308 and ButterflyBVM A100/A108) are to be removed: put replacement resuscitators in place first, then stop using, quarantine and label the units “DO NOT USE”. Earlier lots listed on the page (000081 to 000108, including ButterflyBVM PLUS) stay in use with a pre-use test before every patient use and backup devices on hand. The firm’s letters are dated September 26 and October 7, 2026. The alert states no class. Company contact on the page: info@butterflybvm.com.

ANALYSIS: resuscitators sit in crash carts, vehicles and storage, so the alert’s own advice to search every location before removing units is the main practical point.

Source, accessed 2026-11-09: FDA early alert, Compact Medical

3. Cardinal Health: procedure kits containing recalled BD Alaris infusion sets

DATA: event 100058, posted November 2, 2026 (initiated September 24, 2026). Cardinal Health 200, LLC, Waukegan, IL. Products: Cardinal Health Presource kits containing BD Alaris Pump infusion sets used with the BD Alaris Pump Module Model 8100, in 11 product records covering anesthesia, cardiac catheterization, labor and delivery, cesarean section, IV start and other kits (recall numbers Z-0212-2027 to Z-0222-2027; 716,673 kits and 25,131 units in total as written). Reason as written: “Kits contain infusion sets which were recalled for performance issues.” Root cause: nonconforming material/component. Per the record, Cardinal’s September 24 correction notice asks consignees to quarantine affected kits, alert clinicians, attach a warning label to each kit telling clinicians to remove and discard the recalled component, and return the acknowledgment form. Distribution: U.S. and Canada. Company contact in the record: Cardinal Health Quality Systems, 800-292-9332.

ANALYSIS: this is a kit-level follow-on to a component recall, so hospitals that already acted on the component notice may still hold kits that need labeling.

Source, accessed 2026-11-09: openFDA device/recall, event 100058

4. Intelerad: IntelePACS cybersecurity vulnerability

DATA: event 99971, posted November 4, 2026 (initiated September 10, 2026). Intelerad Medical Systems Incorporated, Montreal, Canada (the record’s action text says “a GE HealthCare Company”). Product: IntelePACS software (product code LLZ, “System, Image Processing, Radiological”; recall number Z-0357-2027; 560 software platforms). Reason as written: a cybersecurity vulnerability that could allow a malicious user to take over an administrator session, delete or modify image or study data, or lock legitimate users out of the system; and a software issue that may cause unintended deletion of image data from an external archive integration. Root cause: software design change. Per the record, a September 15 correction notice says customers can keep using the system under their standard cybersecurity and access-control practices until the corrective update is installed, and asks them to inform users and return the acknowledgment form. Distribution: U.S. (39 states and DC), Australia, Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, South Africa and the United Kingdom.

ANALYSIS: FDA’s Medical Device Cybersecurity page was also updated November 5 (we did not compare versions); this record is a reminder that PACS vulnerabilities are handled through the same recall process as hardware.

Sources, accessed 2026-11-09: openFDA device/recall, event 99971 · CDRH New (cybersecurity page update)

5. Warning letter: Next Medical Products Company (ultrasound gel)

DATA: the FDA table lists a CDRH letter to Next Medical Products Company, LLC, Branchburg, NJ, issued August 19, 2026 and posted November 3, 2026 (subject “CGMP/QSR/Medical Devices/Adulterated”; CMS 727918). The inspection ran November 10 to December 10, 2025. Products: Clear Image ultrasound gel (sterile and non-sterile), MediChoice ultrasound gel and Clear Image Singles. The letter cites environmental controls (21 CFR 820.70(c)), including out-of-specification counts dismissed without investigation; CAPA (820.100(a)); and design changes (820.30(i)) for a label insert and a preservative change that was not verified or validated. It also says the firm did not report to FDA a correction or removal for gel contaminated with Paraburkholderia (21 CFR Part 806), and that the 2024 preservative change required a new 510(k). The letter notes the inspection was conducted under the QS Regulation and that corrective actions must follow the QMSR, in effect since February 2, 2026. It asks for a written response within 15 business days.

ANALYSIS: the letter ties a contamination field action to an unreported correction and an uncleared formulation change, which is a useful example of how one product change can touch design controls, reporting and premarket status at once. We did not review the firm’s response or any later FDA action.

Sources, accessed 2026-11-09: FDA warning letter, Next Medical Products Company · FDA warning-letter table

Also on FDA’s list

DATA: on November 6, 2026 CDRH published a discussion paper, “Evaluating Benefit-Risk in Robotic Medical Devices with Autonomous or Remote Teleoperation Capabilities”, asking for feedback on terminology, benefit-risk considerations for autonomous and for remote teleoperation capabilities, and risk mitigations (docket FDA-2026-N-13135; feedback by January 5, 2027). FDA says it is not draft or final guidance and does not state regulatory expectations. On November 5 FDA updated its QMSR Frequently Asked Questions page; the page does not say what changed. FDA’s CDRH New page also lists four classification notices in the Federal Register on November 4–5: a digital cervical cytology slide imaging system with an artificial intelligence algorithm, prognostic assessment software for mild cognitive impairment, a wearable vibration device for orthopedic use, and an external mechanical stimulator for the relief of congestion (documents 2026-22593, 2026-22590, 2026-22589 and 2026-22657).

ANALYSIS: because the QMSR FAQ update is not described, anyone relying on that page may want to re-read it rather than assume earlier answers still stand. We read only the titles of the classification notices.
