A Class I insulin-pump alarm recall, a heart-valve delivery system and three FDA early alerts

The week in 60 seconds

Recall of the week: Tandem Mobi insulin pumps, Class I

DATA: event 99851, posted October 30, 2026 (initiated September 23, 2026). Recalling firm: Tandem Diabetes Care, Inc., San Diego, CA. Product: Tandem Mobi Insulin Pump, 20 model numbers listed in the record, in 1 product record (product code QFG, device name “Alternate Controller Enabled Insulin Infusion Pump”; 510(k)s include K240309, K223213, K233044, K241078 and K253074; recall number Z-0316-2027). The record says all affected pump models and serial numbers may be affected and lists specific serial numbers with frequent false-positive occlusion alarms that are to be exchanged. Reason as written in the record: certain pumps “may generate false-positive occlusion alarms when no actual insulin flow obstruction is present”; increased friction within the drive module can reduce motor power so that motor speed falls below the occlusion-detection threshold, and the pump may “suspend insulin delivery”; repeated alarms, if not addressed promptly, may interrupt insulin delivery and increase the risk of high blood glucose, which in severe cases may require medical intervention or hospitalization. Root cause as written: process change control. Class: Class I (openFDA device/enforcement, classified October 30, 2026; report date November 11). According to the record, Tandem emailed “URGENT MEDICAL DEVICE CORRECTION” recall and correction notices on September 23, 2026. Users may keep using the pump while they always respond to occlusion alarms and follow the User Guide troubleshooting, keep push notifications on in the Tandem Mobi app, contact Tandem if alarms are frequent, persist or disrupt therapy, keep a backup insulin method ready and complete an online acknowledgement form; owners of the listed serial numbers were told to contact Tandem to arrange an exchange. Quantity: 80,938 (77,559 U.S., 3,379 outside the U.S.). Distribution: worldwide, U.S. nationwide and Canada. The record’s contact line names an individual, so we do not reproduce it; the record’s action text gives Tandem Customer Support, 1-877-801-6901, techsupport@tandemdiabetes.com.

ANALYSIS: the root cause is recorded as process change control and the action is a correction plus exchange of specific serial numbers, so diabetes clinics and pharmacies supporting Mobi users may want to confirm that patients have received the notice and know whether their serial number is on the exchange list.

1. Edwards Lifesciences: SAPIEN M3 Commander M delivery system lots, Class I

DATA: event 100067, posted November 2, 2026 (initiated October 5, 2026). Edwards Lifesciences, LLC, Irvine, CA. Product: Edwards Commander M Delivery System, model 9880CM29A, lots 67718561, 67804014, 67887043 and 67887044 (product code NPU, “Prosthesis, Mitral Valve, Percutaneously Delivered”; PMA P250019; recall number Z-0287-2027; 164 units). Reason as written: “Due to a manufacturing issue which may result in cardiovascular balloon rupturing.” Root cause: process change control. Class: Class I (classified November 2, 2026; report date November 11). Per the record, Edwards sent an “URGENT: MEDICAL DEVICE RECALL (PRODUCT REMOVAL)” letter on October 5 saying that, after complaints, certain lots may contain a defect that may result in balloon burst during use; customers are asked to quarantine and not use affected units, return an acknowledgment form and obtain a return authorization within 15 days; other lots are not affected. Distribution: 24 U.S. states, none outside the U.S. The record’s contact line names an individual; the action text gives US_FCA@edwards.com and Customer Service 1-800-424-3278.

ANALYSIS: only four lots and 164 units are involved, so structural-heart programs can match lot numbers quickly; Edwards was named only in a runner-up list in Issue 05, before this event had a class.

Sources, accessed 2026-11-23: openFDA device/recall, event 100067 · openFDA device/enforcement, event 100067

2. Heraeus Medevio: early alert on vascular guidewires whose coating may detach

DATA: FDA issued this early alert on November 20, 2026. Heraeus Medical Components (doing business as Heraeus Medevio) is removing vascular guidewires, including Navilyst and Spectrum vascular guidewires (H965457461, H965457471) and Arrow Marked Guidewires (PW-18080-HN, PW-16130-HN); FDA links the full product list. The page says the hydrophilic coating “can swell unevenly when hydrated with saline”, causing clumping mostly on the distal coil, and may then flake, displace or detach so that particles could enter the bloodstream; possible harms include thromboembolic events, and FDA notes that reported consequences of hydrophilic polymer embolization range from no symptoms to stroke, organ failure, ischemia or death. As of November 5, 2026, no serious injuries or deaths were reported. Customers are told to immediately stop using and distributing affected products, review inventory and distribution records, quarantine affected lots, inform staff and notify downstream customers. The page states no class. Company contact on the page: MedevioRegulatoryAffairs@heraeus.com, 612-325-2003.

ANALYSIS: the failure description matches the Arrow International alert in Issue 06, and Arrow-branded wires appear on this list too, so facilities that already checked for the Arrow alert may want to check these product numbers as well.

Source, accessed 2026-11-23: FDA early alert, Heraeus Medical Components

3. B. Braun: early alert on Caresite Micro Luer Access Device

DATA: FDA issued this early alert on November 19, 2026. B. Braun Medical Inc. updated use instructions for the Caresite Micro Luer Access Device, component no. S5404000 (FDA links the full product list). The piston in the female end “remained depressed after disconnecting a male luer from the access site”; the suspected cause is insufficient silicone lubricant. Possible harms listed: leakage or backflow of blood, IV fluids or medication, interrupted or delayed therapy, air entry and air embolism, and microbial contamination; the page says neonates, pediatric and critically ill patients may be at greatest risk. As of November 3, 2026, no injuries or deaths were reported. Users should inspect the device after each male-luer disconnection, replace it with aseptic technique if it leaks or the valve stays open (replacing the whole set if the valve is bonded to an extension set), assess patients, inform staff and post the notice; distributors should forward it. The page calls it a “potentially high-risk device issue” and states no class. Company contact on the page: Recalls@bbraunusa.com, 844-903-6417.

ANALYSIS: this is a correction, not a removal, so the burden falls on bedside checks after every disconnection; neonatal and pediatric units may want to brief staff first.

Source, accessed 2026-11-23: FDA early alert, B. Braun Caresite

4. Outset Medical: early alert on Tablo console power connections

DATA: FDA issued this early alert on November 18, 2026 (updated November 19). Outset Medical updated use instructions for the Tablo Console and the TabloCart with Prefiltration Drawer after overheating at the power connection; FDA says “these events occur when the power cord connector is not held firmly in the power inlet.” Affected power cords: PN-0005746 (clear ends) and PN-0008460 (gray ends). Possible harms: first- or second-degree burns, loss of power that delays, interrupts or misses treatment, and fire risk. As of November 10, 2026, no injuries or deaths were reported. Outset’s November 10 letter asks users to inspect the cord before each use, make sure it is fully seated at both ends, use only the Outset-provided cord without extension cords or power strips, stop treatment and unplug when safe if overheating occurs, call Technical Support, and inform staff and home patients. The page calls it a “potentially high-risk device issue” and states no class. Company contact on the page: 844-698-2256, support@outsetmedical.com.

ANALYSIS: Tablo is also used for dialysis at home, so the instructions need to reach patients as well as clinic staff.

Source, accessed 2026-11-23: FDA early alert, Outset Medical Tablo

Expansiones e inversiones

Baja California supera los 76 parques industriales y prevé 20 empresas nuevas; los dispositivos médicos están entre los sectores que busca atraer. DATO: según notas publicadas el 18 de noviembre de 2026 a partir de una presentación de la Secretaría de Economía e Innovación del estado, cuatro parques industriales nuevos (ATISA Park Tijuana South, La Herradura Norte, HubsPark Technology Campus y Parque Industrial Natura) traerían 20 empresas y más de 2,100 empleos. Las notas citan inversión extranjera directa de más de 1,700 millones de dólares en el primer semestre de 2026, más de 430,000 personas empleadas en los parques industriales del estado, y mencionan la planta de Intuitive en Mexicali como inversión estratégica; los sectores que el estado busca atraer incluyen dispositivos médicos, aeroespacial, electrónica, electromovilidad e inteligencia artificial. ANÁLISIS: es una nota industrial general: ninguna de las cifras de empresas o empleos se atribuye específicamente a dispositivos médicos.

Fuentes, consultadas el 2026-11-23: El Tijuanense, 18 de noviembre de 2026 · La Razón, 18 de noviembre de 2026
