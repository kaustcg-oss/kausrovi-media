Norte Recall Pressure Index

How busy was FDA’s medical-device recall desk this month, compared with a normal month of the past year? One number answers it: 100 = a normal month. Above 100, more recall activity than normal; below 100, less. Public openFDA data only.

In 30 seconds

  1. What it counts: recall records FDA posted for medical devices in the month, with Class I (most serious) counted ten times more than Class III (least serious).
  2. What 100 means: the same weighted count as the typical (median) month of the 12 months before.
  3. What it is not: it does not say whether devices are safe, or whether any company is doing well or badly. It is a measure of FDA recall activity, nothing more.

Each bar is one month. Dashed line = a normal month (100). Orange = clearly above normal (over 120), blue = near normal (80–120), grey = quieter than normal (under 80). Faded bar = month still being completed.

Class I, II and III, in plain words

Class I
Reasonable chance of serious injury or death. The most serious class. Counts 10×.
Class II
May cause temporary or medically reversible harm; serious harm is unlikely. Most recalls are here. Counts 3×.
Class III
Unlikely to cause harm (for example, a labeling problem). Counts 1×.

How it is calculated

Each month: W = 10 × Class I + 3 × Class II + 1 × Class III recall records (openFDA device/enforcement, by report date). Index = 100 × W ÷ the median W of the 12 months before. Class I weighs most because it is the highest-risk class. The weights are our editorial choice and are fixed so the series stays comparable.

Worked example

In a month with 30 Class I, 248 Class II and 0 Class III records: W = 10×30 + 3×248 + 0 = 1,044. If the median W of the previous 12 months was 1,039.5, the index is 100 × 1,044 ÷ 1,039.5 = 100.4, a normal month.

Questions people ask

Is a high number bad news?

Not by itself. It means FDA posted more (or more serious) recall records than usual. One large recall with hundreds of product records can lift a month.

Why does the baseline change?

The baseline is always the median of the 12 months before, so it moves as the recent past moves. That keeps the index comparable with “now”, not with an old year.

Can I use it to judge a company?

No. It covers the whole device industry and is not about any one firm. For a company, use the free recall check.

Where does the data come from?

openFDA device enforcement records (public). The exact queries are at the bottom so anyone can reproduce every number.

How to read it, and its limits

  • A record is not an event: one recall can produce many records (one per product or lot).
  • Report date is when FDA classified or posted the recall, not when the problem happened.
  • FDA posts weekly, so months with five posting dates score higher than months with four; that alone can move a month by up to about 25%.
  • The latest month may be incomplete until openFDA refreshes (marked provisional).
  • The index describes recall activity only. It is not a measure of device safety, of any company, or of investment value.
